## Five Whys trace

For K01/K09: data could be exposed/deleted because filesystem/cleanup paths were
trusted; they were trusted because tests used happy-path temp files; tests did
not inject failures because success was judged at exit/artifact level; that
persisted because no actual restore/adverse-path proof was required in CI;
the process therefore needs adversarial fixtures and a retained recovery result,
not merely another safety paragraph.

For K04/K10: a passing gate overstated coverage because it checked a heading or
top-three membership; those proxies were used because fixture provenance and
ownership were not measured; missing measurements hid uncovered skills; release
reports inherited that gap; automation now exposes each metric separately.

For K08: a restart appeared as the fix because the concise example assumed a
particular service version; the example skipped observed upstream identity;
that skipped the causal branch; reviewers saw a complete contract but not the
practitioner output; the repair adds an executable diagnostic/failure case.

Structural rebuilds, complete fleet hardening, every script's runtime lab,
custom SELinux/ACL preservation and cross-engine policy changes are deferred
to [06-next-cycle.md](06-next-cycle.md). No open historical P0 was found; the two
new critical findings are selected here and cannot be closed by scoring alone.
